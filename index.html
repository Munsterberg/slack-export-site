How an export works

  1. Open Slack in Chrome

    Go to app.slack.com and sign in the way you normally do. The extension uses that session.

  2. Click Export

    Use the button in the channel header or thread panel, or choose conversations in the popup.

  3. Choose what to keep

    Pick a format and a date range: all time, the last 30 days, or your own dates.

  4. Get the file

    Your browser fetches the messages from Slack and saves the file to your computer.

Questions

I use the Slack desktop app. Will this work?

Not inside the desktop app, because Chrome extensions only run in Chrome. You don't need to switch for good, though.

Open app.slack.com in Chrome, sign in to the same workspace, and export from there. The desktop app keeps working as normal.

Where do my messages go?

Only to your computer. The extension asks Slack for the messages through your own browser session, builds the file in your browser, and saves it to your Downloads folder. We have no server that receives message content.

The extension sends a few anonymous usage events, such as "an export finished" and which format was used, so we can tell what's working. These never include messages, names, channel names, or workspace names, and you can turn them off in settings. The privacy policy lists every event.

Is this allowed?

The extension only reads conversations you can already open in Slack. It uses your normal sign-in, doesn't get around any permission, and can't post, edit, or delete anything. It saves what you could otherwise copy by hand.

Your workspace may still have rules about keeping copies of messages, especially at work. Check your employer's or client's policy before you export, particularly for conversations that include other people.
